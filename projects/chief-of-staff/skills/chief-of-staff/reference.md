# Chief of Staff — Reference

Load this when maintaining registers, assigning priority, assessing project health, or running a daily/weekly review.

## Canonical records

### 1. Action Register

For every actionable item, record:

* ID
* Action
* Area or project
* Owner
* Status
* Priority
* Due date or expected timeframe
* Source
* Date first identified
* Date last updated
* Next concrete action
* Waiting on
* Relevant context
* Confidence

Statuses:

* `INBOX` — captured but not yet clarified
* `NEXT` — ready for action
* `IN PROGRESS` — actively underway
* `WAITING` — dependent on another person or event
* `SCHEDULED` — assigned to a future date
* `BLOCKED` — cannot proceed without resolving an obstacle
* `SOMEDAY` — intentionally deferred
* `DONE` — completion supported by evidence
* `DROPPED` — deliberately abandoned

### 2. Open Loops Register

Track unresolved matters that may not yet be conventional tasks:

* unanswered messages
* decisions awaiting resolution
* promised follow-ups
* approvals needed
* external responses expected
* unresolved questions
* work started but not closed
* projects with no recent movement
* meetings without captured follow-ups
* conflicting or unclear instructions

Each open loop must include why it remains open and the smallest action that would move or close it.

### 3. Decision Register

Record material decisions with:

* decision
* date
* decision-maker
* rationale
* affected projects
* resulting actions
* assumptions
* conditions that would justify revisiting it

Do not label a suggestion or working direction as a decision unless it was explicitly accepted.

### 4. Waiting-For Register

Record:

* who or what is being waited on
* what is expected
* date requested
* expected response date, if known
* follow-up date
* consequence of continued delay

### 5. Ideas and Incubator

Keep unapproved ideas separate from committed work. Include enough context to evaluate later, but do not repeatedly surface them as overdue work.

## Prioritization

Assess items using:

* consequence of delay
* real deadline
* people blocked
* strategic importance
* financial, legal, security, or reputational risk
* ability to unlock other work
* effort required
* whether the item has become stale

Priority levels:

* `P0` — immediate safety, security, legal, financial, or production concern
* `P1` — urgent or materially blocking
* `P2` — important and should be advanced soon
* `P3` — worthwhile but deferrable
* `P4` — optional, exploratory, or someday

Do not mark everything urgent. When priorities compete, explain the tradeoff and recommend an order.

## Daily and weekly behavior

### Daily review

* Focus on new information, urgent changes, today’s commitments, messages needing replies, and blocked work
* Keep the briefing short and actionable
* Do not repeat unchanged background

### Weekly review

* Reconcile all accessible sources
* Review every active project and open loop
* Identify stale work and commitments
* Confirm what was completed
* Recommend what to advance, schedule, delegate, defer, or drop
* Surface patterns such as overloaded priorities, repeated blockers, or projects losing momentum
* Propose a realistic focus for the coming week

## Project health

For each active project, maintain:

* objective
* current status
* most recent meaningful progress
* next milestone
* next action
* owner
* blockers
* decisions needed
* outstanding communications
* risks
* last activity date

Flag a project as potentially stale when it has active commitments but no meaningful movement. Do not infer failure merely from inactivity; explain what evidence is missing.
